Monthly mathematics announcements on arXiv

New papers in every mathematics subject class, counted by the month arXiv announced them and harvested from the arXiv API. Each paper is counted once, under the primary category its author chose.

Method

Every record with a math.* category was fetched from the arXiv API, then counted in the month arXiv announced it. That month is the YYMM at the start of the paper's identifier, so 2609.01234 was announced in September 2026. Only new papers count: a replacement version keeps its identifier and is never counted again. Counts use each paper's primary category, so a paper appears exactly once and the columns sum to the field total. Cross-listed appearances are excluded.

A class is said to rise or fall when its last twelve months differ from the twelve before by more than twice the counting noise, that is by more than 2√(a + b) for the two totals a and b. Smaller changes are reported as flat: a class with about ten papers a month can move 15% between two years by chance alone.

Reading the panels

With All months selected each panel is a monthly line. Pick a single month instead and each panel becomes one column per year, so the same month is compared across years with no seasonal confound — mathematics has a strong seasonal cycle, and comparing like with like removes it.

Caveats

Counts cannot establish cause. A rise in new papers is consistent with more researchers, faster writing, thinner slicing of results, or more mathematics — and this data separates none of those.

Every month shown is final. arXiv fixes a paper's identifier at the 14:00 ET cutoff of the batch it goes out in, and a paper held for moderation gets the identifier of the month it is finally released. A late paper therefore adds to the month it appears in and never changes an earlier one, so the newest month needs no allowance for stragglers. One boundary detail: a batch cut off on a month's last Friday goes public on Sunday evening, which can fall in the next calendar month. It still counts in the month of its identifier.

math.IT and math.MP are absent because arXiv stores them under their canonical names cs.IT and math-ph; they never appear as a primary math.* category. math.GM is where moderators route submissions that miss a class's bar, so its rise reads as a quality signal, not a productivity one.
